Breaking the Shame Culture: Why Silence Enables Institutional Harm

የድርበት ثقافት መሰበሰብ፡ ስለምን silence institutional harmን ያቆጣጠራል

📅 May 15, 2026 ✍️ PALM Editorial Team ⏱ 6 min read

In many Ethiopian religious communities, shame has become a powerful tool of control — not merely a personal emotion, but a structural force that silences victims, protects abusers, and preserves harmful institutions.

The Cost of Silence

When survivors of institutional abuse are told to "keep quiet for the sake of the church," the damage extends far beyond the individual. Entire communities learn that accountability is optional and that power without transparency is acceptable.

"True transformation requires leaders who do not just manage institutions, but actively advocate for justice, peace, and structural health."

A Path Forward

PALM's Advocacy for the Abused sector provides a confidential, trauma-informed pathway for those seeking healing. We partner with legal experts and the Inter-Religious Council of Ethiopian Evangelical Churches to push for stronger safety policies.

  • Confidential, encrypted support channels for survivors
  • Trauma-informed counseling and pastoral care
  • Policy advocacy for institutional accountability
  • Community education to replace shame with honest dialogue

Breaking the shame culture is not about destroying institutions — it is about restoring them to health through gospel truth, justice, and compassion.
